# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences, roughly equal weight:
- **Solo/unsupervised athletes** training BJJ without a coach or structured class, using the site's games and curriculum to self-teach via the constraints-led approach.
- **Coaches & instructors** pulling games and lesson structures to build and run their own class plans.

Both come to the site to browse and read structured content (games, lesson plans, blog posts) — there's no account system, dashboard, or app workflow. The site is the resource itself.

## Product Purpose

Increase the accessibility of submission grappling by publishing free, structured training content: constrained games organized by position, pre-built lesson plans combining them into curricula, and educational writing on the constraints-led approach and ecological dynamics. Success looks like an athlete or coach with no formal instruction being able to run a full session, or build one, entirely from the site.

## Brand Personality

Scrappy, accessible, no-gatekeeping. This is a grassroots, DIY resource — the opposite of a paywalled certification or a polished gym-membership funnel. It should feel like a generous training partner sharing what they know, not a brand selling itself.

## Anti-references

- **No generic SaaS/startup look** — no gradient-hero, rounded-card, bold-CTA startup-marketing aesthetic. This isn't a product with a subscription funnel.
- **No combat-sports bro aesthetic** — no skulls, black-and-red aggression, tribal fonts, or macho/predator imagery (explicitly: no lion/apex-predator visual cliches). The content is scientific and collaborative, not dominance-themed.
- **No corporate polish** — avoid anything that reads as an institutional or franchise gym brand.

## Design Principles

- **Show, don't gatekeep** — content and structure should make training runnable without a coach in the room; clarity beats mystique.
- **Scientific confidence, not machismo** — the constraints-led approach and ecological dynamics framing is the credibility source; let rigor read through structure and clarity, not aggressive visual signaling.
- **Generous, not salesy** — the site gives away real training content for free; nothing should look like it's building toward a paywall or upsell.
- **Content-first hierarchy** — games, lesson plans, and articles are the product; layout should serve fast scanning and reading, not decorative flourish.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, don't rely on color alone to convey information. No further unusual requirements specified.
